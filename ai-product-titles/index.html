How to Write Product Titles with AI (Free Prompts)

By Seller Notebook · Updated November 2026

In short

  • Find real keywords first: type your product into the Etsy or Amazon search bar and read the suggestions. That is what buyers actually type.
  • Keep only the keywords that are true for your product.
  • Give the AI your facts and keywords, and ask for 3–5 title options.
  • Check and edit the title yourself: AI adds details, repeats words and ignores its own rules.

Title best practices: what a good title does

A product title has two jobs: help the listing show up in search, and make the buyer want to click. A good title:

  • Starts with what the item is, in the words a buyer would use ("black cat mug", not "Midnight Whiskers").
  • Adds the one or two details buyers search for and choose by, like material, colour, style or who it's for.
  • Reads like a normal phrase, not a list of keywords glued together.

AI is good at the third part: turning a few facts into several clean versions to choose from. It is bad at knowing which words buyers search for, and at sticking to only the facts you gave it. This guide covers both.

Keywords: how much do they matter?

A lot, but not in the way "SEO titles" sometimes suggest. Keywords decide whether your listing can show up for a search at all. If buyers type "black cat mug" and your title says "Midnight Whiskers Cup", the search engine has less to match.

But keywords don't make anyone click. A title stuffed with every possible phrase looks like spam to buyers, and some platforms have rules against it. Amazon, for example, has rules about repeating words in titles.

The balance that works:

  • One main keyword at the start: the simplest way a buyer would describe the item.
  • One or two supporting keywords that are true and useful: material, style, recipient.
  • Everything else goes elsewhere: Etsy tags, Amazon bullet points, the description.
Don't ask AI to "invent SEO keywords". It guesses. Real keywords come from what buyers type, and the next steps show you where to find them for free.

Step 1: Write down your product facts

Before you open any AI tool, list the facts. The AI can only be as accurate as what you give it.

  • What the item is (mug, necklace, print, candle)
  • Material
  • Colour and design
  • Style (cute, minimalist, vintage)
  • Size, only if you know it exactly
  • Who it's for or what occasion it suits

Leave out anything you're not sure about. Don't know how much your mug holds? Then don't write "large" or "16 oz", even if buyers search for it.

Step 2: Find real keywords for free

The free way to see what buyers type is the search bar itself:

  1. Go to Etsy or Amazon.
  2. Type the simplest name for your item, for example "cat mug", and don't press Enter.
  3. Read the suggestions that drop down. These come from real searches.
  4. Try a second, more specific version, for example "black cat mug".

Then do the most important part: cross out every suggestion that isn't true for your product. The suggestions show what people want, not what you sell.

Suggestions change over time and can differ by country and by person, so check them yourself for your own products.

Paid keyword tools can show search volumes, but the search bar is enough to start. For more free ways, see How to Find Etsy Keywords for Free.

Step 3: Ask the AI for title options

Paste your facts and the keywords you kept into this prompt. You can also make it with the free AI title generator in our prompt builder: choose "Product title".

Prompt: title options
Write 5 product title options for my [Etsy / Amazon / Shopify] listing.

Product facts:
[paste your facts]

Keywords buyers search for (all true for this product):
[paste the keywords you kept]

Each title should:
- start with what the item is, using the main keyword
- include the one or two details buyers are most likely to search for
- read naturally
- fit the platform's current title length limit

Rules:
- Use only the facts above. Don't invent size, capacity, materials or features.
- No keyword stuffing and no repeated words.
- After the titles, tell me in one line which one you would pick and why.

Ask for 3–5 options. With fewer you have little to choose from, and with more you mostly get near-copies.

Step 4: Adjust for Etsy, Amazon or Shopify

Each platform has its own title rules, and they change. Add one line to the prompt for the platform you sell on, and check the official rules before you publish.

  • Etsy: titles can be up to 140 characters. Keep them easy for people to read. Put the main keyword in the first few words. On phones, buyers often see only the start of a title. Extra phrases go in your 13 tags, not the title.
    Add to the prompt: "Keep it easy to read and put the most important words in the first 40 characters."
  • Amazon: Amazon has been tightening its title rules, including limits on repeated words. Several seller news sites report a 75-character limit for most categories from July 2026. Check the current limit for your category in Seller Central.
    Add to the prompt: "Keep it under [current limit] characters and don't use any word more than twice."
  • Shopify (your own store): there's no marketplace search. But the title becomes your page heading, and often your title in Google. Keep it clear and short.
    Add to the prompt: "Write it as the page heading of an online store, short and clear."

Example: a black cat mug

We ran this method on an example product in November 2026. The search suggestions and AI answers are real. The AI answers were made with Claude and are shown exactly as they came out.

Product facts: white ceramic mug, black cat design, cute style, a gift for cat owners. We don't know its exact capacity, so we left size out.

The search bar suggestions

  • Etsy, "black cat mug": handmade, personalized, ceramic, png
  • Amazon, "black cat mug": halloween, for cat lovers, with ears, microwave safe, cute, large, 16 oz, for men, judging you silently

Most of these are not true for our mug. It isn't handmade or personalized. It has no ears. We don't know its size, or if it's microwave safe. We kept only black cat mug, ceramic, cute and for cat lovers.

What the AI suggested for Etsy

AI titleWhat's wrong with it
Black Cat Mug, Cute White Ceramic Coffee Mug, Gift for Cat Owners"Coffee" isn't in the facts. The AI assumed it.
Cute Cat Mug with Black Cat Design, White Ceramic Cup for Cat LoversUses "cat" three times, although the prompt said no repeated words. Starts with "Cute", not the item.
White Ceramic Mug with Black Cat, Cute Cat Owner GiftFine, but the main keyword "black cat mug" is split up.
Black Cat Coffee Mug, Cute Ceramic Gift for Cat Moms and Cat Dads"Coffee" again, "cat" three times, and "cat moms and cat dads" is the AI's own idea.
Cat Lover Mug, White Ceramic with Cute Black Cat PrintStarts with "Cat Lover Mug", which is not how most buyers search.

The AI picked the first title "because it starts with the phrase buyers search for". That's a good reason, but it missed its own invented "coffee".

What the AI suggested for Amazon

AI titleWhat's wrong with it
Cute Black Cat Mug, White Ceramic Gift for Cat Owners (53 characters)Starts with "Cute" instead of the item.
White Ceramic Mug with Black Cat Design, Gift for Cat Lovers (60 characters)Good facts, but the main keyword isn't at the start.
Black Cat Ceramic Mug, Cute Cat Owner Gift (42 characters)Short and accurate. "Cat owner gift" is less natural than "gift for cat lovers", which buyers actually search.

The final title, edited by hand

Black Cat Mug in White Ceramic, Cute Gift for Cat Lovers (56 characters)

  • Starts with the main keyword, "black cat mug".
  • Uses only true details: white, ceramic, cute.
  • Ends with a real search phrase, "gift for cat lovers".
  • No invented facts, and "cat" appears only twice.
  • Short enough for both Etsy and the reported Amazon limit.

All the AI options were usable starting points. None was ready to publish without a check.

Step 5: Check the title before you publish

Read the title once with your fact sheet next to it:

  • No invented details. Look for words you didn't give the AI ("coffee", "large", "handmade", "gift box").
  • No repeated words, or as few as possible. Count the main word.
  • The item comes first, not an adjective like "cute" or "beautiful".
  • No stuffed keywords. If you can't read it out loud naturally, cut it.
  • No banned symbols or claims, such as "best seller", "free shipping", "#1" or all-caps words. Check your platform's rules.
  • Within the current length limit for your platform and category.

Follow-up prompts

If a title is close but not quite right, ask the AI to fix one thing at a time:

Prompt: fix one title
Here is my product title:
[paste the title]

My product facts:
[paste your facts]

Please:
- remove any detail that isn't in my facts
- make sure no word appears more than twice
- start the title with "[your main keyword]"
- keep it under [number] characters

Give me 3 versions. Don't add new information.

Other short follow-ups that work well:

  • "Make option 2 shorter without losing the main keyword."
  • "Which words in this title are not in my facts?"
  • "Rewrite it so it sounds less like a list of keywords."

FAQ

How long should a product title be?

Long enough to say what the item is and the one or two details buyers search for, and no longer. Each platform has its own limit, and limits change. Check the current one in the Etsy Seller Handbook or Amazon Seller Central. Buyers often see only the first part of a title, especially on phones, so the most important words go first.

Should the main keyword come first?

Yes, where it reads naturally. Start with the words a buyer would type, such as "black cat mug". Then add the details that help them choose.

Can I use the same title on Etsy and Amazon?

You can if it fits both platforms' rules. A short, clear title usually works on both. Check each platform's current length limit and title rules before you copy it over.

How many title ideas should I ask for?

Three to five. Fewer gives you little to choose from, and more tends to produce near-copies of the same title. Pick the best one, then edit it yourself.
